# Engine Follow-ups

Open engine work: gaps, deferred decisions, harness debt and actions outside the repo. A deferred decision leaves this file in the same change that records the maintainer's answer, for RESEARCH.md's Decisions Log if the code doesn't show its reason; a gap leaves with the PR that fixes it.

Pick work by demand, not by sweeping this list (RESEARCH.md, Engineering), and work depth-first, finishing a change under way before starting new discovery (RESEARCH.md, Docs). Demand shows in the `Real usage` headings of `harness/accepted/<browser>.txt`, the failures from the harness's real-usage sample, whose draws follow how often apps lay out each kind of text (harness/README.md, Two kinds of set); By real-usage impact, below, orders the gaps by them. Most entries under Gaps matter only under 24px or in adversarial text, and the list is ordered by mechanism, not by weight.

Terms used below:
- The Chromium (or Blink), WebKit and Gecko profiles are Pretext's rules for Chrome, Safari and Firefox, picked from the user agent (`getEngineProfile()` in `src/measurement.ts`); the scans are their ports of each engine's break-opportunity scan (`src/line-breaks.ts`, `src/gecko-line-breaks.ts`).
- webkit-host is the system WebKit that installed Safari runs, which the harness drives in a background app (harness/README.md, Browsers and pins).
- The stand-in Canvas is the harness's fake measurer (`harness/invariants.ts`, used by `bun harness equal --offline`): it gives characters made-up widths from a formula, not a font's, so a result on it says nothing about shaping.
- Main before #340 is commit 6d1d2106 (2026-09-24), the last main whose break rules were Pretext's own rather than ports of the engines' line breakers.
- The old test suite is `tests/wrapping`, which the harness replaced (#341) and which was removed on 2026-09-25 (#348); its counts stay only where they carry a decision.
- The per-engine rebuild is branch `rebuild-20260916`: a from-scratch port of each engine's line breaking, never shipped, kept as the plain-text correctness reference.
- Issue #321 is the public write-up of an offline study that emulated each engine's line layout (2026-09-15); entries cite its numbered decisions and side findings.

A size such as `633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128` counts the harness cases holding the shape at b1fd05fc, then those of them on Chrome's / Firefox's / webkit-host's accepted list. Dates say when a behavior was seen, and "by" a date means on or before it: in Chrome 153, Firefox 155 and Safari 26.5.2 until 2026-09-16, then Firefox 156.0 and Safari 27.0, then the pins of 2026-09-25 (`harness/pins.json`); a Safari sighting from before 2026-09-16 is unconfirmed on Safari 27 unless a webkit-host case holds it.

Before starting one:
- Rules whose errors cancel land together, in layers with a replay after each: #225's broader attempts lost old-suite cases that main then passed only because two of its errors, the U+3000 hang and controls before brackets, cancelled out (RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends).
- A prerequisite written here can be wrong: this file once said Firefox's breaks between styled pieces needed a model of its word segmentation, and turning on Chrome's joined-text rule for the Gecko profile sufficed.
- A fix may be on for one engine and off for another only when the off engine's losses come from a model named here as missing.

## Gaps

### Kerning Chrome's Canvas doesn't report

Chrome's layout shapes a line whole and its Canvas word by word, cut at each space and around each CJK character (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Chrome), so the page applies pair kerning that no Canvas width holds. The Chromium profile takes the kerning with spaces from Canvas another way (Kerning with spaces, below); between kana it follows Canvas and charges the line more than Chrome lays out. Firefox's page shapes words apart too and turns kerning off in CJK text (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Firefox), and Safari's kerning with a following space is in the WebKit profile (RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges) and its page doesn't kern the kana below, so neither has the gap. Firefox does kern across a soft hyphen, where Pretext cuts a word, as Chrome does (Line edges). Main before #340 failed these cases too.
- Between kana. Hiragino Sans, Hiragino Mincho ProN and Hiragino Maru Gothic ProN kern kana pairs (GPOS `kern`), where PingFang SC and Osaka don't (16px `ファイルをアップロードしてください` is 268.80, 268.64 and 267.84px on Chrome's page against Canvas's 272px, and equal in the other two): in 16px Hiragino Sans, Chrome lays `キス` out 31.05px wide and that pair eight times 244.96px, where Canvas gives each character 16px, 256px in all, as Firefox and Safari lay it out; `って` kerns too, `テキ` doesn't, and Canvas does report a kana's kerning with a mark that stays in its Canvas word (`です。` is 46.4px in both). Chrome so fits a character more on some lines: the sample's `下人は、老婆が…` in 12px Hiragino Sans at 717.5px puts 60 characters in 716.16px, which are 720px unkerned. These are all 15 cases under "Real usage: Japanese and Chinese lines" on Chrome's list, 14 of them a break on another character and 1 a wrong line count: 9 of the sample's 192 cases with kana in a Hiragino Sans list, 5 of 48 in a Hiragino Mincho ProN list and 1 of 32 in a Meiryo list, which macOS falls back from to Hiragino. It is also why keep-all groups break otherwise (Fitting arithmetic). Canvas's default mode starts a new word at each kana (`NextWordEndIndex`, `plain_text_node.cc:93-155`, Chromium 153), so no string measures two of them together; whether `textRendering = 'optimizeLegibility'` measures a Hiragino string whole, as it does in fonts whose lookups involve the space glyph (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Chrome), wasn't tried, and is where a fix starts (the widths are from `bun harness explain` in Chrome 154.0.8037.57, 2026-09-30; that kerning accounts for each case, from shaping the recordings' lines offline with the fonts' own tables).
- Across spaces. The Chromium profile takes this kerning since #408 (Kerning with spaces, below, has what it leaves; RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges, has the rates before and after). It fixed the 10 cases that were under "Windows-only font first in the list", "Roboto and Inter" and "Indonesian in Arial and Bulgarian in Times New Roman" on Chrome's list, and a letter-spaced Times New Roman paragraph. An 11th case under those headings wasn't kerning: at −0.2px letter spacing the browsers lay 16px Roboto's `Notifikasi` out without its fi ligature, 66.0px wide in each, where Pretext added the spacing to Canvas's width with the ligature, 65.45px. It passes in Chrome and Firefox since #397 and stays on webkit-host's list (Letter spacing).

### Bidi levels, direction and script runs

Pretext takes no paragraph direction and resolves no bidi levels (RESEARCH.md, Bidi Levels). Without levels:
- WebKit keeps punctuation after an overflowing first character with it to the end of its item, which a level change ends, and the profile to the end of the scan's segment (`אב((tail` on a left-to-right page; 633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128, most under 24px). WebKit also asks its scan again where levels change (`ab””tail` in a right-to-left paragraph; 15 positions; 2026-09-23).
- A mark at another bidi level than the word or number before it is shaped apart from it on the page, where Canvas measures the two as one string. Each engine cuts its text where the resolved level changes before it shapes, and takes the paragraph's level from the block's `direction`: Blink splits the item (`SegmentBidiRuns`, `inline_node.cc:1333-1436`, over `InlineItem::SetBidiLevel`, `inline_item.cc:207-254`) and ends a shaping run where the direction changes (`ShouldBreakShapingBeforeText`, `inline_node.cc:472-490`; Chromium 153), WebKit splits the text item (`breakAndComputeBidiLevels`, `InlineItemsBuilder.cpp:637-728`; WebKit 7625.1.29.11.27), and Gecko makes a text frame of each level run (below). So the page has no kerning between two characters that a level run ends between. In a right-to-left paragraph those are a Latin word or a number and the full stop, comma or other neutral character after it, where right-to-left text, a line feed or the paragraph's end follows the mark, which then takes the paragraph's level (UAX #9 N1, N2). Canvas resolves the string it is given as a paragraph of its own, left-to-right unless the context says otherwise (Blink, `plain_text_node.cc:293-362`; Gecko, `CanvasRenderingContext2D.cpp:5130-5147`, `:5261-5263`), and WebKit's resolves no levels (`CanvasRenderingContext2DBase.cpp:3064-3070`), so each measures the pair kerned. ### Emergency breaks inside a word

- Each engine fits an overlong word its own way (#195, #421): Chrome by right-context positions, Safari by line-start prefixes, Firefox by the advances of the word shaped whole. ### Kerning with spaces

- A line that ends at a word that kerns with the space after it.
