# Engine Follow-ups

Open engine work: gaps, deferred decisions, harness debt and actions outside the repo. A deferred decision leaves this file in the same change that records the maintainer's answer, for RESEARCH.md's Decisions Log if the code doesn't show its reason; a gap leaves with the PR that fixes it.

Pick work by demand, not by sweeping this list (RESEARCH.md, Engineering), and work depth-first, finishing a change under way before starting new discovery (RESEARCH.md, Docs). Demand shows in the `Real usage` headings of `harness/accepted/<browser>.txt`, the failures from the harness's real-usage sample, whose draws follow how often apps lay out each kind of text (harness/README.md, Two kinds of set); By real-usage impact, below, orders the gaps by them. Most entries under Gaps matter only under 24px or in adversarial text, and the list is ordered by mechanism, not by weight.

Terms used below:
- The Chromium (or Blink), WebKit and Gecko profiles are Pretext's rules for Chrome, Safari and Firefox, picked from the user agent (`getEngineProfile()` in `src/measurement.ts`); the scans are their ports of each engine's break-opportunity scan (`src/line-breaks.ts`, `src/gecko-line-breaks.ts`).
- webkit-host is the system WebKit that installed Safari runs, which the harness drives in a background app (harness/README.md, Browsers and pins).
- The stand-in Canvas is the harness's fake measurer (`harness/invariants.ts`, used by `bun harness equal --offline`): it gives characters made-up widths from a formula, not a font's, so a result on it says nothing about shaping.
- Main before #340 is commit 6d1d2106 (2026-09-24), the last main whose break rules were Pretext's own rather than ports of the engines' line breakers.
- The old test suite is `tests/wrapping`, which the harness replaced (#341) and which was removed on 2026-09-25 (#348); its counts stay only where they carry a decision.
- The per-engine rebuild is branch `rebuild-20260916`: a from-scratch port of each engine's line breaking, never shipped, kept as the plain-text correctness reference.
- Issue #321 is the public write-up of an offline study that emulated each engine's line layout (2026-09-15); entries cite its numbered decisions and side findings.

A size such as `633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128` counts the harness cases holding the shape at b1fd05fc, then those of them on Chrome's / Firefox's / webkit-host's accepted list. Dates say when a behavior was seen, and "by" a date means on or before it: in Chrome 153, Firefox 155 and Safari 26.5.2 until 2026-09-16, then Firefox 156.0 and Safari 27.0, then the pins of 2026-09-25 (`harness/pins.json`); a Safari sighting from before 2026-09-16 is unconfirmed on Safari 27 unless a webkit-host case holds it.

Before starting one:
- Rules whose errors cancel land together, in layers with a replay after each: #225's broader attempts lost old-suite cases that main then passed only because two of its errors, the U+3000 hang and controls before brackets, cancelled out (RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends).
- A prerequisite written here can be wrong: this file once said Firefox's breaks between styled pieces needed a model of its word segmentation, and turning on Chrome's joined-text rule for the Gecko profile sufficed.
- A fix may be on for one engine and off for another only when the off engine's losses come from a model named here as missing.

## Gaps

### Kerning Chrome's Canvas doesn't report

Chrome's layout shapes a line whole and its Canvas word by word, cut at each space and around each CJK character (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Chrome), so the page applies pair kerning that no Canvas width holds. The Chromium profile takes the kerning with spaces and the kerning between kana from Canvas another way (Kerning with spaces and Kerning between kana, below). Firefox's page shapes words apart too and turns kerning off in CJK text (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Firefox), and Safari's kerning with a following space is in the WebKit profile (RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges) and its page doesn't kern the kana below, so neither has the gap. Main before #340 failed these cases too.
- Between kana. The Chromium profile takes this kerning since #412 (Kerning between kana, below, has what it leaves; RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges, has the fonts, the model and the cost). Hiragino Sans, Hiragino Mincho ProN and 17 more Japanese faces of macOS kern kana pairs, where PingFang SC and Osaka don't: in 16px Hiragino Sans, Chrome lays `ファイルをアップロードしてください` out 268.80px wide and `キス` 31.05px, where Canvas gives each character 16px, as Firefox and Safari lay them out. Chrome so fit a character more on some lines: the sample's `下人は、老婆が…` in 12px Hiragino Sans at 717.5px puts 60 characters in 716.16px, which are 720px unkerned. That was all 15 cases under "Real usage: Japanese and Chinese lines" on Chrome's list, 14 of them a break on another character and 1 a wrong line count: 9 of the sample's 192 cases with kana in a Hiragino Sans list, 5 of 48 in a Hiragino Mincho ProN list and 1 of 32 in a Meiryo list, which macOS falls back from to Hiragino. They pass now, as do 12 engine facts and rich cases and 2 sample paragraphs in `system-ui` lists.
- Across spaces. The Chromium profile takes this kerning since #408 (Kerning with spaces, below, has what it leaves; RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges, has the rates before and after). It fixed the 10 cases that were under "Windows-only font first in the list", "Roboto and Inter" and "Indonesian in Arial and Bulgarian in Times New Roman" on Chrome's list, and a letter-spaced Times New Roman paragraph. An 11th case under those headings wasn't kerning: at −0.2px letter spacing the browsers lay 16px Roboto's `Notifikasi` out without its fi ligature, 66.0px wide in each, where Pretext added the spacing to Canvas's width with the ligature, 65.45px. It passes in Chrome and Firefox since #397 and stays on webkit-host's list (Letter spacing).

### Letter spacing

- Cursive scripts (Arabic, Syriac, N'Ko, Mandaic, Mongolian, Phags-pa, Hanifi Rohingya) take no letter spacing in the Blink and Gecko profiles, as in Chrome since 149 and in Firefox (#397; `src/prepare.ts`). A tab after such text lands on the browser's stop with it: `name`, tab, `مرحبا بالعالم`, tab, `ok` in 15px Times New Roman at 1px and 172px, which wrapped while the Arabic was charged 2px too much, is one line, 169px wide, in Firefox 156.0.1, in Chrome 154 and in Pretext (2026-10-01). From 138 to 148 a cursive run takes no letter spacing at all, its spaces and no-break spaces included (`? !is_cursive_script : true`): there the profile is too wide by the spacing times the run's spaces, 3.5px over an 8-word line at 0.5px, where main was too wide by the spacing times its letters and spaces. From 149 the run's spaces and no-break spaces are spaced (`!is_cursive_script || treat_as_space`), which is what the profile ports. The first step is Chromium 77ab8bb837 (2025-04-02), in 137 with the flag experimental, which is off by default, and 97e135f47a (2025-05-07, after 137 branched) made the flag stable: release tags 137.0.7151.55 and .119 have it experimental and 138.0.7204.49 stable, though Chrome's release notes list the rule under 137; whether Chrome turned it on in 137 from its servers isn't known. ### Bidi levels, direction and script runs

Pretext takes no paragraph direction and resolves no bidi levels (RESEARCH.md, Bidi Levels). Without levels:
- WebKit keeps punctuation after an overflowing first character with it to the end of its item, which a level change ends, and the profile to the end of the scan's segment (`אב((tail` on a left-to-right page; 633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128, most under 24px). WebKit also asks its scan again where levels change (`ab””tail` in a right-to-left paragraph; 15 positions; 2026-09-23).
- Neutral characters measure in their neighbour's script: Chrome's `(` is 6.12px alone and 11px in an Arabic run. Safari paints a right-to-left `）` after Arabic at 9px, not 16px, so at 12-18px it keeps the U+202F after `中（ابب）` on that line, where Pretext gives it one of its own (67 cases; 14 / 13 / 4; by 2026-09-12). Chrome's page gives a run of neutral Latin-1 characters, such as `)` × 15 or `«»` × 8, the script before it, and its Canvas measures it as Latin, so in Amiri and Noto Naskh Arabic such a run after Arabic or Han paints wider than Pretext charges (RESEARCH.md, String Storage, 2026-09-27). On a page with `lang="en"`, Firefox paints `.` and `:` after an Arabic word in a right-to-left block narrower than its Canvas measures them, by 0.93 and 1.2px in 15px Noto Naskh Arabic; with `lang="ar"` the two agree. Of 519 Arabic and Urdu corpus paragraphs in that font at 22 widths of 200-368px, 128 of 11,418 line counts are wrong there without letter spacing and 123 at -0.2px, lines that were right before #397 only while the spacing charged to joined letters covered the difference; with `lang="ar"`, or in 16px Arial, none is (Firefox 156.0.1, 2026-10-01; the cause isn't traced).
- Chrome measures under `<html dir>` as it was when the context was made, so text against the page's direction wraps slightly differently around brackets. A `direction` option fixed it in a prototype and wasn't taken (RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends); it's on the list for the API discussion planned for the end of the project (TODO.md; 2026-09-13).
- In a right-to-left paragraph Safari breaks `src/|עברית` and the WebKit scan doesn't; not a case (2026-09-14, Safari 26.5.2).

The line APIs called once for a paragraph lay it out as an unbounded width (`normalizeMaxWidth()`, #401); `layoutNextLine()`, `layoutNextLineRange()` and `layoutNextRichInlineLineRange()` are called once for each line, and with the comparison in them Chrome's mixed stream row read 1.4-11.2% slower in each of five sessions; with the check in the clamp each line loop already makes, which covered the streams, Firefox's mixed line and resize rows read 1.7-10.2% slower in each of three (#409, closed; RESEARCH.md, Decisions Log, 2026-10-02). A stream always returns at such a width, and `NaN` and `undefined` give the same lines. Each admission asks whether a segment overflows the line, which nothing does at `NaN`, so the lines break as at an unbounded width. Three places ask whether something fits, which nothing does either, and there a streamed line differs from the one at `Infinity`:
  - A pre-wrap line that ends, before a newline or the text's end, in spaces, or in tabs where the profile hangs them (`hangTabs`: Blink's and WebKit's, not Gecko's), reports a width of `NaN`, in plain text and rich inline: what hangs is clamped to the available width with `Math.min` (`walkPreparedComplexLines()`, `stepRichInlineLine()`). A Gecko-profile line that ends in a tab counts the tab and reports its width. The line's range is the same.
  - In the Blink and Gecko profiles on desktop, a line that starts inside a segment holding an invisible character, such as a ZWNJ or a word joiner, ends after one grapheme, where at `Infinity` it takes the rest of the text. An earlier line at a real width leaves such a start. It differs only from a start whose widths to the segment's end were measured apart (the segment's entry geometry has an entry for that grapheme), since `getFreshLineEnd()` (`src/entry-geometry.ts`) asks whether they fit; other starts in such a segment take the rest.
  - Rich inline takes an item whole where it fits and walks it otherwise (`stepRichInlineLine()`), so at `NaN` it walks every item. In the Gecko profile the walk starts the fragment of an item that begins with soft hyphens after them, where the item taken whole starts at them: the same text and width from another start cursor.

  Of 5,932 plain and 2,068 rich cases drawn from the sets, on the stand-in Canvas in each of the four profiles, the first differs for 15-17 plain and 35 rich cases, the second for 94 and 103 plain and 8 and 46 rich in the Gecko and Blink profiles, and the third for 9 rich in the Gecko profile; no other difference showed, and the six APIs called once for a paragraph differ for none (2026-10-02). An app that streams before its container is measured can pass `Infinity`. Whether such a width throws instead, as a `letterSpacing` that isn't finite does (#356), is on the API discussion's list (TODO.md).
- A start cursor that is no position in the text is checked by no stream. In `layoutNextLine()` and `layoutNextLineRange()` a segment index of `NaN` never returns on text with a soft hyphen, nor `-Infinity` on any text; -1 gives a line that starts with `undefined`; a grapheme index past its segment's end, as a cursor kept from a longer text can be, gives a line of width `NaN`, and one inside a space throws a `TypeError`. `layoutNextRichInlineLineRange()` never returns from an item index of `-Infinity`. And `measureLineStats()`, `walkLineRanges()` and `layoutWithLines()` never return on a JSON copy of a handle, as the walkers count segments to the length of a typed array, which the copy lacks (CHANGELOG, #340). Fixes for both are on branch `plain-hardening` (`0c0ad8d5`, `ff3576f5`, `27c1f6a0`), which stays unmerged and undeleted while this entry points at it. They were left out of #401 for what they cost in the bench: the two cursor checks made Chrome's mixed stream row 5% and then 11% slower, and counting segments by `widths` made Safari's pre-wrap layout 3.6-5.1% slower (2 sessions each, Chrome 154.0.8037.57 and Safari 27.0, 2026-10-01). A form that costs those rows nothing reopens them, or the maintainer's word that the checks are worth the cost.
