# Engine Follow-ups

Open engine work: decisions for the maintainer, known gaps and harness debt.

## Decisions

- Decide on other Canvas font settings (#107), including whether a kerning-enabled Canvas is viable: Chromium layout kerns across spaces, ZWSP and soft hyphens, but default Canvas doesn't report that kerning. README says Pretext assumes default font kerning; #199 and #216 stay open in case Safari's OffscreenCanvas ever follows `fontKerning`.
- Decide whether `prepareRichInline()` supports `whiteSpace: 'pre-wrap'` (#173, #193). Accepting it needs a native styled-inline pre-wrap oracle.
- Revisit what rich-text editing needs from Pretext: source offsets through whitespace normalization (#90) and caret positions (#198), and whether bidi selection and copy/paste behavior stay outside this package. Do a pass over the open demo and showcase issues (#94, #99, #150, #167).

## Line breaking

- Chrome, Safari and Firefox take break opportunities from ports of Blink's, WebKit's and Gecko's scans over their own tables (`src/line-breaks.ts`, `src/gecko-line-breaks.ts`), and engines Pretext doesn't recognize take Blink's. Items below that name a break rule were found on the merged segmentation the scans replaced. Those whose examples the scans now answer as the browsers do were dropped; recheck the rest against the installed suite on the scans before closing them.
- Chrome opens `line_normal_cj.brk` for `zh` content, and for content without a language under a Chinese UI, where the double curly quotes act as brackets and `〜` and `゠` can start a line. The Blink scan opens it from `<html lang>`, and on a page without one from the language `Intl` shows as its default, which is Chrome's UI language. It doesn't see an element's own `lang`, including `lang=""`, which Chrome reads as no language, or a `Content-Language` header or `<meta http-equiv>`: under a Chinese UI, `<html lang=en><p lang="">中文””tail` breaks before `tail` in Chrome and not in Pretext. DevTools locale emulation, which Playwright's `locale` option uses, changes `Intl`'s default but not Blink's.
- Blink restarts ICU at each line start, which drops the context before the line, where the scan reads each text once. On the suite data that changes 86 of 188,274 verdicts, each one unit after a line start: a combining mark after U+3000 or TAB, a curly quote between CJK characters, and `-` before a digit.
- WebKit splits items where bidi levels change and asks its scan again at the split, and Pretext resolves no levels. Without them the WebKit scan differs at 71 positions in 71 right-to-left suite requests: U+3000 before `b`, CR or FF before Latin letters, a TAB before a space, closing curly quotes before Latin letters (`ab””tail`), and under keep-all punctuation before Latin letters (`אב((tail word`).
- The WebKit profile follows Safari 27 only. Safari 26, on macOS 26 and iOS 26 until they update, breaks curly quotes and guillemets as ICU does, breaks keep-all text only at spaces, doesn't end a line at U+2028 or U+2029, and breaks after an overflowing first character whatever follows it. The profile doesn't detect the version: only Safari's own user agent names one.
- Safari resolves `serif`, `sans-serif`, `cursive`, `fantasy` and `monospace` to the family Core Text names for the content language wherever WebKit's script for it isn't Common (FontDescriptionCocoa.cpp:77-118), and its Canvas fonts carry no language. ## Widths, shaping and emergency breaks

- Blink's shaping-cluster overflow units change no suite rows and help only letter-spaced complex scripts. A result-identical plain-text screen exists, but V8 builds 172 script regexes on first use, adding about 26-66ms to the first complex-script preparations on a page. - Measure emergency fits in context, per engine: Chrome by right-context positions, Safari by line-start prefixes, Firefox by shaped advances (#195). The Gecko profile takes line-start prefixes as Safari's does in segments at least 80px wide, and standalone graphemes in narrower ones, so below 80px it fits emergency breaks inside joined or kerned words as the Blink profile does (RESEARCH.md). A boundary that splits a cluster misses by more: in items `မြန်မာဘာသ` and `ာသည်လှပသောဘာသာဖြစ်သည်`, the second starts with U+102C, which shapes with the consonant before it, and in 16px Myanmar Sangam MN `ဘာသ` and `ာသည်` measure 91.80px separately but 82.03px joined. Chrome's rich prediction then starts a line at that boundary where its spans don't, and in Firefox the joined rule adds a line at 20 of 52 probed widths, including the `maintained/rich-boundaries` Myanmar row's 88px, which breaking at every item boundary matched by accident. Consider a prepare-time boundary correction for Blink and Gecko.
- Rich-inline layout clamps the line width and an item's available width to at least 1px, while the plain line walkers clamp to 0, so widths below 1px lay out differently in rich-inline.
- Rich-inline layout charges an item's `extraWidth` on every piece of an item split across lines, where browsers pad only the start of the first piece and the end of the last. A `600 13px Menlo` item `src/layout.ts` with 12px of `extraWidth`, between `the file (` and `) is the entry`, takes a line more than Firefox at 46-48px and 82-86px, and than Chrome at 46-48px.
- Rich-inline layout loses a collapsed space before an item holding only a soft hyphen. Items `a `, U+00AD and `b` lay out one space narrower than `a ` and `b`, and `b`'s fragment has no gap (`gapItemIndex` -1): the soft-hyphen item isn't line content, so its fragment gets no gap, and it ends the pending space. Browsers should keep that space; not yet probed.
- Rich-inline layout misses the break after a soft hyphen that starts an item after other content on the line. The item's walk consumes that soft hyphen as a line start and splits the word after it instead, so line ends move back as the width grows. In the test fake canvas, items `u`, `\u00ADzv` and `uo` give `uz` / `vu` / `o` at 19.2-28.7px and `u` / `zvu` / `o` from 28.8px, where the flat walker gives `u-` / `zv` / `uo`, then `u-` / `zvu` / `o`. Items `nmdo`, `\u00ADau` and `o a` give `nmdoa` / `uo a` at 48-57.5px and `nmdo` / `auo a` at 57.6px.
- Rich-inline layout paints and charges no hyphen for a soft hyphen that ends an item where a line breaks: items `ab\u00AD` and `cd` give `ab` / `cd` at 19.2-38.3px, where the flat walker gives `ab-` / `cd`.
- In the Chromium profile, rich-inline layout returns from an unfit hyphen only to the break before the item, not to an earlier soft hyphen inside an earlier item, as the flat walker would. Where the item follows a soft hyphen that ends the previous item and its first letter is narrower than the hyphen, the line keeps the overflowing hyphen until the break before the item leaves room for it, then ends there, so the line's end moves back as the width grows. A seeded search with a tabulated fake canvas found this for items `pa\u00ADd\u00AD`, `a\u00ADm` and `e\u00ADa om`, where the flat walker returns to the first soft hyphen instead.

## Per-browser gaps

## Cost

Same-document timings in Chrome 153, Firefox 156 and Safari 27.0 against main (`tests/wrapping/VALIDATION.md` at 6fadbe5).

- The simple stepper takes every segment boundary as a break, so the line APIs lay out text with a boundary the scan doesn't break at, as before NEL, a C0 or C1 control, or U+2028 or U+2029 where they don't end a line, and in Firefox after a space before a bidi control, with the full walker; `layout()` counts it with the stepper and hands the full walker only the lines the stepper ends at such a boundary. The line APIs could do the same, but where a line's space overflows its width can move in the last bits, since the stepper sums the widths before the space and the full walker subtracts the space from the sum after it: 99 of 96,470 offline line checks in the Gecko profile move, and no harness prediction in any browser (RESEARCH.md, Keeping Work Bounded). Making the full walker sum them too would move its widths the same way on the text only it lays out. Text with zero-width glue, as around marks after a ZWSP or soft hyphen, takes the full walker in every API.
- Once `layout()` has counted such text with the simple stepper, Chrome's `walkLineRanges()` of simple text, which shares the stepper, takes 2 to 4% more time than a second copy of main, by a mechanism not found. A private copy of the stepper for the count, 77 more lines, is the only measured cure; it isn't in, since a few percent of JIT cost doesn't pay for duplicated code (RESEARCH.md, Keeping Work Bounded).
- The full walker costs about three times the counter per segment in Chrome and Safari and five times in Firefox, which is why simple text keeps its own walkers (RESEARCH.md, Keeping Work Bounded). Letter-spaced CJK, a segment per character, is still `layout()`'s costliest text per character, at about half of main's time.
- Since grapheme clusters come from the character rules, Firefox prepares CJK text seen before about 2 to 4% slower: the Gecko scan runs the rules over every word with a unit at or above U+0300, where its `Intl.Segmenter` probes skipped Han words that never join. A table of the probes' answers built from the rules gave 1.01 of main's speed, within the noise (RESEARCH.md, Grapheme Clusters From Engine Data).
- A run of combining marks after zero-width glue or a control is measured after the grapheme before it and the fewest of the chain's last runs that hold 96 UTF-16 units, so a chain prepares in time linear in its length (#351), but every run's context still holds that whole grapheme: `x` with 4,000 U+0301, then 4,000 pairs of a control and a mark, submits 33 million units to Canvas offline, half of main's 64 million.
- Firefox resolves UAX #9 levels for every text that holds a right-to-left character, about 150 to 200ns a unit, which keeps Arabic chat messages seen before at 1.18 times main's `prepare()` there. Taking allocations and closures out of the port gained under 6%.
