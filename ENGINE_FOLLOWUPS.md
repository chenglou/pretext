# Engine Follow-ups

Open engine work: gaps, deferred decisions, harness debt and actions outside the repo. A deferred decision leaves this file in the same change that records the maintainer's answer, for RESEARCH.md's Decisions Log if the code doesn't show its reason; a gap leaves with the PR that fixes it.

Pick work by demand, not by sweeping this list (RESEARCH.md, Engineering), and work depth-first, finishing a change under way before starting new discovery (RESEARCH.md, Docs). Demand shows in the `Real usage` headings of `harness/accepted/<browser>.txt`, the failures from the harness's real-usage sample, whose draws follow how often apps lay out each kind of text (harness/README.md, Two kinds of set); at b1fd05fc (main on 2026-09-27) the largest were WebKit's soft hyphens, Chrome's Japanese and Chinese lines, and Arabic and Urdu with letter spacing. Most entries below matter only under 24px or in adversarial text.

Terms used below:
- The Chromium (or Blink), WebKit and Gecko profiles are Pretext's rules for Chrome, Safari and Firefox, picked from the user agent (`getEngineProfile()` in `src/measurement.ts`); the scans are their ports of each engine's break-opportunity scan (`src/line-breaks.ts`, `src/gecko-line-breaks.ts`).
- webkit-host is the system WebKit that installed Safari runs, which the harness drives in a background app (harness/README.md, Browsers and pins).
- The stand-in Canvas is the harness's fake measurer (`harness/invariants.ts`, used by `bun harness equal --offline`): it gives characters made-up widths from a formula, not a font's, so a result on it says nothing about shaping.
- Main before #340 is commit 6d1d2106 (2026-09-24), the last main whose break rules were Pretext's own rather than ports of the engines' line breakers.
- The old test suite is `tests/wrapping`, which the harness replaced (#341) and which was removed on 2026-09-25 (#348); its counts stay only where they carry a decision.
- The per-engine rebuild is branch `rebuild-20260916`: a from-scratch port of each engine's line breaking, never shipped, kept as the plain-text correctness reference.
- Issue #321 is the public write-up of an offline study that emulated each engine's line layout (2026-09-15); entries cite its numbered decisions and side findings.

A size such as `633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128` counts the harness cases holding the shape at b1fd05fc, then those of them on Chrome's / Firefox's / webkit-host's accepted list. Dates say when a behavior was seen, and "by" a date means on or before it: in Chrome 153, Firefox 155 and Safari 26.5.2 until 2026-09-16, then Firefox 156.0 and Safari 27.0, then the pins of 2026-09-25 (`harness/pins.json`); a Safari sighting from before 2026-09-16 is unconfirmed on Safari 27 unless a webkit-host case holds it.

Before starting one:
- Rules whose errors cancel land together, in layers with a replay after each: #225's broader attempts lost old-suite cases that main then passed only because two of its errors, the U+3000 hang and controls before brackets, cancelled out (RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends).
- A prerequisite written here can be wrong: this file once said Firefox's breaks between styled pieces needed a model of its word segmentation, and turning on Chrome's joined-text rule for the Gecko profile sufficed.
- A fix may be on for one engine and off for another only when the off engine's losses come from a model named here as missing.

## Gaps

### Letter spacing

- Blink gives cursive-script runs no letter spacing except on spaces, Firefox none between joined Arabic letters, and Chrome none to U+202F (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts); Pretext spaces every character. Real text shows it ("Arabic and Urdu with letter-spacing" on Chrome's and Firefox's lists): at 1.5px letter spacing in 16px Arial both paint `ابب` in `中（ابب）` 4.5px narrower than Pretext charges. Chrome before 149 lacks the rule or applies it differently, so a fix picks a version gate or a README line. #321's side finding 3 (by 2026-09-12).
- Firefox resolves letter spacing to whole app units, 1/60px (`ResolveLetterSpacing`, `nsTextFrame.cpp:1949-1962`), where Pretext takes it as given: at −0.08px, as Signal Desktop sets Inter, each character takes −5/60px, so in 14px Inter `I also want code fences, quotes` is 207.22px wide in Firefox and 207.32px in Pretext, and a word fits that much sooner (3 Firefox cases of the rich set's `negative-letter-spacing` family, main too; 2026-09-29). Spacing that is a whole number of app units, such as −0.2, −0.5 and −1px, matches; em-based spacing often isn't one (−0.01em at 17px is −0.17px). A Gecko-profile rule that rounds the spacing looks cheap; it moves every Gecko width under such spacing, so it wants a gate of its own.
- Characters with no advance take no gap in any engine, each by its own unit (in Blink per shaping cluster, in WebKit only on glyphs with an advance, in Firefox per cluster and none after a format character): with 1px letter spacing, two word joiners (WJ) at width 1px in 16px Arial are one line in all three browsers and split in Pretext (by 2026-09-12; attempts in RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends). A fix needs one per-grapheme spacing unit every rule shares, with per-segment arrays only where needed. Waiting on it: WebKit's return from a soft hyphen whose hyphen doesn't fit (`unfitHyphenRetreat`); a ZWSP right after a forced break inside a word, which all three browsers give a line of its own (it also waits on joined Arabic widths); Blink's shaping-cluster overflow units.
- Tab stops: the profiles' stops leave out the letter spacing Chrome's and Firefox's count; every tab gets its own gap, which Firefox gives only a tab ending its text run, as one before Arabic does; and Firefox's minimum of half a `0` is missing, while the WebKit profile's half a space (`tabStopAdvance / 16`) goes wrong once stops count spacing (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts; 270 pre-wrap cases with a tab and letter spacing; 55 / 132 / 17). The errors can cancel, so some passes are luck: port the interval, the minimum and the gap in layers with a replay after each, and pin exact stop multiples in the unit test (43.6px isn't exact in binary). Blink's half-space skip is a separate gap. #321's side finding 2 (by 2026-09-12).

### Bidi levels, direction and script runs

Pretext takes no paragraph direction; only the Gecko scan resolves levels, taking every paragraph as left-to-right (RESEARCH.md, Bidi Levels; what that premise moved is under Break Opportunities From Engine Data). Without levels:
- WebKit keeps punctuation after an overflowing first character with it to the end of its item, which a level change ends, and the profile to the end of the scan's segment (`אב((tail` on a left-to-right page; 633 cases; 124 / 73 / 128, most under 24px). WebKit also asks its scan again where levels change (`ab””tail` in a right-to-left paragraph; 15 positions; 2026-09-23).
- Neutral characters measure in their neighbour's script: Chrome's `(` is 6.12px alone and 11px in an Arabic run. Safari paints a right-to-left `）` after Arabic at 9px, not 16px, so at 12-18px it keeps the U+202F after `中（ابب）` on that line, where Pretext gives it one of its own (67 cases; 14 / 13 / 4; by 2026-09-12). Chrome's page gives a run of neutral Latin-1 characters, such as `)` × 15 or `«»` × 8, the script before it, and its Canvas measures it as Latin, so in Amiri and Noto Naskh Arabic such a run after Arabic or Han paints wider than Pretext charges (RESEARCH.md, String Storage, 2026-09-27).
- Chrome measures under `<html dir>` as it was when the context was made, so text against the page's direction wraps slightly differently around brackets. A `direction` option fixed it in a prototype and wasn't taken (RESEARCH.md, Dead Ends); it's on the list for the API discussion planned for the end of the project (TODO.md; 2026-09-13).
- In a right-to-left paragraph Safari breaks `src/|עברית` and the WebKit scan doesn't; not a case (2026-09-14, Safari 26.5.2).
- Unwitnessed: whether `direction: rtl` alone turns on Firefox's document bidi, which decides where the Gecko scan must resolve levels.
- The Gecko scan doesn't split text runs where the script changes (RESEARCH.md, Decisions Log, 2026-09-24). Of the two fuzz strings in `harness/cases/followups.ndjson`, the `ko` one (Firefox 3 lines, the profile 4) is on Firefox's list; the `th` one passes in Firefox and is on Chrome's list for an untraced reason: Chrome keeps its final U+2007 on the last line, the library on a line of its own.

### Emergency breaks inside a word

- Each engine fits an overlong word its own way (#195): Chrome by right-context positions, Safari by line-start prefixes, Firefox by the advances of the word shaped whole. The Blink profile sums standalone graphemes: at 248px, `'ه'.repeat(140)` in `400 14px Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` fits 62 isolated letters a line, Chrome 44 joined ones (2026-09-14). A mark that can't start a line sends the word before it through these fits (`foo@bar.com` before `，b`, where Pretext splits off `m`; 19 cases; 1 / 0 / 0). The WebKit profile fits past 96 graphemes from pairs (96 `ه` break where Safari does, 97 don't). The Gecko profile sums standalone graphemes in segments under 80px, a premise whose gap at 24-80px is measured in RESEARCH.md, Break Opportunities From Engine Data (RESEARCH.md, Decisions Log, 2026-09-27).
- Chrome keeps kerning across an overflowing word's break, which Canvas can't show (RESEARCH.md, Kerning At Line Edges), and Safari carries a broken word's remaining width to the next line unmeasured, which needs a Safari fit model that loses nothing (`'AV'.repeat(17)`: 3 lines, not 4, in 36 of 36 headless WebKit checks, 2026-09-15; RESEARCH.md, Safari (WebKit)). Neither is a case.
- In narrow boxes Safari keeps two joined Arabic graphemes on a line where Pretext splits them (by 2026-09-12, Safari 26.5.2).
- Splits inside a segment take Unicode graphemes; Firefox takes its own cluster starts, keeping a Myanmar spacing mark such as U+102C with the cluster before it, though its `Intl.Segmenter` splits them (by 2026-09-12, Firefox 155). Open: whether Firefox's other cluster starts beyond ICU4X's grapheme rules (a leading extender continues a cluster, a Bengali YA joins after a VIRAMA) undo the Gecko profile's use of Chrome's grapheme table (RESEARCH.md, Engine Facts, Firefox).
- Arabic joined across a soft hyphen is measured at isolated widths. For the Gecko profile, a ZWJ on each connected letter, gated by a check that a split's two sides add up, matched Firefox 155 on 1,458 of 1,576 Noto Naskh Arabic widths and can't reach Amiri or Noto Nastaliq Urdu (2026-09-12; RESEARCH.md, Content Language And Fonts). Prototype it with the Firefox halves of the rules that wait on joined Arabic widths, such as the ZWSP after a forced break under Letter spacing.

### Negative letter spacing and hanging spaces

- At −6 in pre-wrap the browsers don't fit a word after a hanging space, and Pretext does (` A B` in 16px Arial; 7 cases; 3 / 0 / 1; 2026-09-15).
- Chrome ends a line before a space after a soft hyphen, where Pretext hangs the space (`a`, U+00AD, space, `b`; 161 cases; 50 / 2 / 25; 2026-09-15).
- At −1 in pre-wrap, Safari and Firefox give the space after `لا` in `a لا ب` (1px, 32px Arial) a line of its own, where Pretext hangs it (2026-09-15, Safari 26.5.2).

- Where a run between breaks overflows the line and negative advances after it bring the text's width back under the line's, rich inline takes an item that fits whole onto its line, as Blink takes a text item whole where its shaped width fits (`ShapingLineBreaker::ShapeLine`, `shaping_line_breaker.cc:281-297`, Chromium 153), and the plain-text walker ends the line at that run, as WebKit does, which fits each run between breaks in turn (`TextOnlySimpleLineBuilder.cpp:318`, `InlineLineBuilder.cpp:1585`). - Pretext disagrees with itself: text laid out again at its widest line can break earlier, since a space, a ZWSP and a soft hyphen fit before the negative gap the reported width includes (Gecko profile, stand-in Canvas: `x.ywordy\t\t foo` in pre-wrap at −1 is 83.48px wide at 99px, and at 83.48px its second tab moves down; 2026-09-15, rechecked at b1fd05fc). ### Rich-inline item edges

Since #369 (2026-09-27) an item's walk continues the line (RESEARCH.md, Rich Inline Boundaries, Continuing The Line), which fixed the soft-hyphen and U+2028 shapes this list held before: a soft hyphen that starts an item after other content is a break, an item of only a soft hyphen keeps the collapsed space before it, and an item holding a U+2028 or U+2029 that the WebKit scan makes a hard break ends its line there. What's left comes mostly from items measured apart, from white space beside soft hyphens and bidi controls at an item's edge, and from spans that browsers lay out otherwise than their text in one node. Counts from 2026-09-27 and 28 are of probes (RESEARCH.md, Rich Inline Boundaries) in Chrome 154, Firefox 156.0.1 and webkit-host.

### Fitting arithmetic

Each engine's line fit is exact in its own units (RESEARCH.md, Measurement Model); the Blink and Gecko profiles fit with a 0.005px allowance instead, a named gap, not a tolerance to tune. Its cost sits under the "Within 1/64 px of where the browser's lines change" headings, most on Chrome's list; Chrome's real-usage Japanese and Chinese lines, which fit one more character than the Canvas widths add up to, may be the same (untraced).
